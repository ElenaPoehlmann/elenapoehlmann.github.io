Elena Pöhlmann

Job market paper

Drawing on original field data from networks in Germany, Austria and Switzerland, I examine how admission rules, interaction formats and digital platforms shape members’ ability and willingness to recommend one another for consequential professional opportunities. The paper explains how networks organize conditions for brokering while enabling direct contact with recruiters and decision-makers.

Aleksandra Gregorič, Elena Pöhlmann, and Marie Louise Mors

Offering rare insights into the Danish executive recruiting industry, we combine search-process panel data with recruiter interviews to examine how clients’ gender diversity expectations shape candidate pools and sourcing practices. We show how recruiters position women considered on potential alongside candidates presented as proven or safe, distinguishing eligibility from comparative credibility.

Elena Pöhlmann, Catarina Marques dos Santos, and Jermain Kaminski

Can imagining an entrepreneurial future make it feel more attainable for students in higher education? Two experiments compare audio-guided simulations focused on concrete, near-term activities with more distant, outcome-focused reflection. We examine psychological distance and entrepreneurial intention, finding that the experimental contrast does not consistently change these outcomes across studies. The paper explores the possibilities and limits of brief cognitive interventions in entrepreneurship education.

Elena Pöhlmann

Data collection

I examine through a whole-network survey of a leadership network in Germany how members’ relational histories are associated with support leading to career outcomes, including executive roles, board appointments and collaborations. The study distinguishes outcomes attributed to particular members from those associated with the network infrastructure.
